TL;DR

Analysts certify Genie Agents so business users know which ones to trust. But the badge has nothing behind it, and it doesn't notice when the agent changes. In my trial I certified an agent, then told it to round revenue to the nearest $1,000. The benchmarks failed. The badge stayed Certified. Then Improve with Genie swapped out two of my benchmarks, and it was still Certified.

The fix: Certified means "checked, on this version." Save who certified which version, and what the results were. Turn the badge amber when someone makes an edit that can change answers. Then give the owner a one-click re-check that compares result tables with the certified version, with no LLM doing the grading. Now is the time: Databricks wants everyone building and sharing agents, AI helpers now make many of the edits, and Genie stops being free on January 31, 2027. Success is 80% of certified agents still on their certified version after 90 days.

Prototype: https://shatakshi107.github.io/genie-certification-prototype/

Problem, Opportunity, and Evidence

Who and when: Priya (fictional) is a BI analyst at Harbor Health. She builds a Genie Agent on governed tables, certifies it, and shares it with a sales team. Afterward she, a teammate, or Genie's AI helper keeps editing it, while business users keep picking it because it says Certified. Priya wants it to stay trustworthy as it improves. The sales team wants to act on an answer without checking it themselves.

This grading noise is a known LLM-judge problem, not a Genie bug. LLM judges disagree with themselves across repeated runs (R1), and a production text-to-SQL judge reached only κ = 0.42 agreement with human labels on a random sample (R2). My tests prove it happens. The beta measures how often.

Why now

ScalePepsiCo is moving about 30k reports to about 50 Genie agents, and GM runs hundreds (Data + AI Summit 2026 keynote), and Genie Code writes about 60% of LakeFlow pipelines. More people and AI will edit trusted agents.
AI reachSince June 2026, Genie One prioritizes certified assets (Databricks release notes), so a stale badge also steers AI answers.
TimingGenie usage is free through January 31, 2027 (Databricks release notes), so trust built now will affect usage once billing starts.

Why it's worth doing: Fewer wrong numbers reach leadership. Genie is billed by use ($0.07/DBU), so trust turns directly into queries. Power BI and Snowflake both let teams endorse or verify content, but from their docs, neither shows what changed in the answers (R9). It also reuses benchmarks people already wrote.

Jobs to Be Done

Core job (analyst): ensure an AI data assistant gives my business team correct answers, before and after it is shared. Desired outcomes, ranked:

  1. Minimize the likelihood that business users rely on an agent that changed after it was checked. (P0-2)
  2. Minimize the time it takes to see whether a change altered answers people already trust. (P0-3)
  3. Minimize the likelihood that tests or settings change without my explicit approval. (P1-2)
  4. Minimize the effort to show stakeholders who checked an agent, when, and on what. (P0-1)

Related job (business user): decide quickly whether I can act on an answer without re-checking it.

Key decisions

DecisionWhyTrade-off
No score gateVerdicts flip, so good agents would fail at random.Relies on human judgment, so I track whether analysts review changes.
Compare tables instead of gradingComparing two tables gives the same result every time.It can't say whether a change is good. That call stays with the analyst.
Lapse by type of editInstructions, SQL, tables, joins and benchmarks lapse it. Titles and sharing don't.Fewer false alarms, but someone has to maintain the list.
Amber instead of removing the badgeRemoving it would alarm users and hide history.Users can learn to ignore amber, so a guardrail tracks it.
Benchmarks in v1, live traffic laterCheaper, and avoids privacy issues.Only covers those questions.

Top alternative: require a passing benchmark score to certify

Requirements

IDRequirement and acceptance test
P0-1Receipt: Certifying stores certifier, time, version, benchmark results and a note. With no benchmarks, the badge says "no tests attached." Test: all appear in the dialog and on hover.
P0-2Lapse: Any listed edit, including by the AI helper, turns the badge amber in the header, Agents list, Discover and chat. Test: editing an instruction turns it amber by the next page load; editing the title doesn't.
P0-3Re-check: One click re-runs certified questions, marks each result table Changed or Same, and shows what changed in the configuration and who changed it. Test: 8 questions finish within 2 minutes, with progress shown, at no more than the cost of one benchmark run.
P1-1Revert with preview: Show certified and current answers side by side before confirming. V1 only reverts to the certified version.
P1-2AI approval: A timed-out or skipped prompt applies no change. AI edits to benchmarks need explicit approval.
P1-3Safer "Update ground truth": Show what it replaces, and keep the original expected answer (T6).
P1-4Flag flips, and ask instead of guessing: Tag questions whose verdict changed across runs or modes. If one is ambiguous, ask the analyst to choose a meaning and save it (R3).
P2Full version history. Audit entries showing "proposed by Genie Code, approved by [user]." Real-question replay.

Out of scope: grader accuracy, model determinism, draft vs published agents, and object types beyond Genie Agents.

Measurement and rollout: Certification events and versions come from system tables. Launch as a private preview with 5–10 design partners to set the thresholds, then turn it on for new certifications, then existing ones. Long term, certified agents should get fewer thumbs-down than uncertified ones. What would change the plan: if fewer than 20% of amber agents get re-checked within a week, owners don't act on the badge, and the signal moves to a direct alert to the owner instead.
